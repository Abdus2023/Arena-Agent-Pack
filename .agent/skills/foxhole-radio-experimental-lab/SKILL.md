# Foxhole Radio Experimental Lab

## Purpose

Convert historical foxhole-radio designs and razor-blade/graphite detector hypotheses into reproducible, safe experiments. Separate demonstrated receiver function from unproven microscopic explanations.

## Activation

Use when reconstructing a foxhole radio, testing a razor-blade/graphite contact, comparing crystal detectors, or documenting passive AM reception and RF harvesting.

Do not use to claim that every blade/graphite contact is a Schottky diode, that a blue surface identifies a particular oxide, or that successful AM reception proves a microscopic mechanism.

## Inputs and preconditions

- Sample identity and provenance.
- Blade material and surface treatment, if known.
- Graphite grade and contact geometry.
- Antenna, ground, tuned circuit, source impedance, and load.
- Instrument identity, calibration state, and measurement uncertainty.
- Safety review for sharp metal, outdoor antennas, and RF equipment.

## Procedure

1. Record historical source claims separately from present-day measurements.
2. Photograph and label each sample; do not infer oxide chemistry from color.
3. Secure the blade in an insulating fixture with exposed edges guarded. Adjust the graphite contact without handling the blade edge.
4. Characterize DC I–V in both polarities using a current-limited source. Record raw readings, range, temperature, contact force, position, and repeats.
5. Test contact repeatability after repositioning and reassembly.
6. Characterize RF response with a calibrated signal source, known reference plane, controlled input power, frequency, and load.
7. Test AM demodulation with a known carrier, audio tone, and modulation depth. Compare recovered audio against the reference.
8. Integrate the detector with an LC tuned circuit only after standalone detector behavior is measured.
9. Compare against reference detectors under matched source, frequency, power, modulation, and load conditions.
10. Record negative results, noise floor, loading, drift, and uncertainty.
11. Treat Schottky barrier, tunneling, oxide conduction, microcontact networks, and thermal effects as competing hypotheses.
12. Use SEM/EDS, XPS, or AFM only where available and appropriate; microscopy/chemistry alone does not prove the electrical mechanism.
13. Publish separate conclusions for function, performance, and microscopic mechanism.

## Outputs

- Sample registry and fixture specification.
- Raw DC/RF/AM measurements.
- Analysis with units and uncertainty.
- Detector comparison report.
- Evidence ledger and unresolved mechanism hypotheses.
- Rebuild documentation and safety record.

## Failure handling

Stop if the blade cannot be secured safely, measurement current limits are unavailable, instrument calibration is unknown for a quantitative claim, antenna installation is unsafe, or data are insufficient to distinguish the claimed behavior. Mark unmeasured properties OPEN.

## Safety constraints

- Never connect an antenna to mains wiring.
- Disconnect outdoor antennas during thunderstorms and use appropriate grounding/protection.
- Guard sharp blade edges and avoid exposed hand contact.
- Do not heat or chemically treat blades without a controlled procedure and appropriate safety equipment.
- Do not claim a passive receiver has no energy source: received RF energy from the transmitter powers the detected signal.

## Acceptance criteria

- Sample, fixture, source, load, and instruments are documented.
- DC nonlinearity is distinguished from RF rectification.
- RF response is measured at a known input level.
- AM recovery is compared with a known reference.
- Comparison conditions are controlled.
- Function and microscopic mechanism have separate evidence statuses.
- Historical claims are cited and current measurements are reproducible.
